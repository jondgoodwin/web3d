The world-wide web that Tim Berners-Lee wove in CERN’s lab over twenty years ago now stretches around the globe, encompassing billions of colorful, interactive, interconnected pages offered up by millions of independent sources. With these pages, one can do so many things: chat, share, buy, sell, play, explore, learn, plan, coordinate, and so much more. These pages, with all their color and capability, have become a pervasive and integral part of our daily lives.

But these pages, they are flat! What if we could immerse ourselves within three-dimensional virtual worlds, where we can explore, play, socialize and make things happen in a way that feels like a more natural extension to the three dimensional world we already inhabit. This is already true for many gamers and product design, but is largely not realized in other domains. Given the opportunity, many people might want the ability to learn using 3d models and simulations, walk around and explore the past, present or future/fantasy worlds built by others, design their own virtual vacation home visited by friends and family, shop for items whose shape and placement matters, monitor and control complex systems or organisms, and so much more. The extra dimension has the ability to add needed information, realism and engagement to so many situations.

To a large degree, the necessary hardware and software technology to deliver 3d worlds already exists or is coming soon (e.g. virtual reality headsets and sensors). What is missing are a robust collection of open 3d standards that enable anyone full access to any 3-d world across the Internet, comparable to the standards that form the backbone of the world wide web. What we have instead is many closed islands of proprietary technology and a few inadequate and insufficiently used standards.

What standards do we need? In general, just two types of standards: the network protocol and the 3-d world modeling language. Complying with (and extending) these standards would grow a collection of competing, proprietary 3D browsers and server technologies that cooperate to deliver any user to any 3-d world on demand.

For the most part, we can piggyback on the Internet’s existing network protocols regarding URL addressing, domain lookup, resource exchange (HTTP), encrypted communications (SSL), and even data synchronization (UDP). The new open standards we require are those for the 3-D world modeling language.
