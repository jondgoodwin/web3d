The core requirements that the open 3D web standards need to support:

Open. The standards need to be owned by the industry at large, who can evolve them forward in a collaborative manner by all significant stakeholders. Where possible, it is also better if the key reference software technologies be open source with relaxed copyright licenses. In the absence of a single dominant player, openness is the only way to avoid creating unconnected islands of 3-d content.

Modular. The standard should make it easy to assemble a world using parts or services scattered across Internet servers, each identified by its URL. This feature lowers the barrier to profitability for both world- and specialized part-builders. The larger the ecosystem, the more everyone benefits.

Hyperlinked. The standard should make it easy to incorporate and use portals that transfer a user easily from one world to another URL-defined world. Having worlds be interconnected will increase user engagement and excitement, increasing the value of a world and enabling the creation of portals, search tools and aggregators.

Interactive. The standard should encourage creation of dynamic worlds full of motion and complex behaviors which the user can interact with in engaging ways, hopefully in pursuit of meaningful goals. A static world with nothing to do rarely holds one’s interest for long. Enabling interactivity lengthens user engagement and desire to return, improving a world’s ability to recoup its cost to build.

Social. The standard should facilitate creation of multi-user worlds. Social engagement with other people around the world can increase user return rates and engagement.

Stateful. The standards should make it easy to evolve the state of a world and its users based on accumulated interactions and the passage of time. Adding variety and accumulated accomplishments makes a world more exciting to stay in or return to.

Responsive. The standard and the implementations need to avoid multi-second lags between most user requests and the world’s responses. Long delays break immersion and increase frustration and annoyance, causing users to quit or return less often.

Flexible. The standard should not just make common world building activities easy and similar, but should enable creation of worlds that choose a different path. By allowing both consistency and flexibility, users can gracefully move from world to world balancing their needs for familiarity and variety as best meets their needs.
